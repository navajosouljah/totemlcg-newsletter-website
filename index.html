Every investment category that ever reshaped how capital moves started in the same place: invisible to the market, dismissed as niche, and quietly working for years before the world caught up. Index funds were called "Bogle's folly" for over a decade. Private credit was treated as a backwater until the 2008 financial crisis forced it into the mainstream. REITs sat dormant from 1960 until institutional money finally arrived in the 1990s. The structural truth in each case was the same the whole time. The story took longer to land than the math required.

We think Totem sits in that exact position right now.

For the last several years, we have been quietly building a category of capital deployment that, as far as we know, does not exist anywhere else. A contracted, insured, transaction-level commercial loan structure tied to verified Fortune 100 purchase orders. Repaid on a 90 to 100 day cycle. Both principal and returns insured by Allianz, the largest trade credit insurer in the world. Verifiable on a phone call before any capital moves.

The proof is real. Over 2,000 completed transactions. Zero defaults. Several million dollars of partner capital deployed. A growing list of Fortune 100 buyers behind every transaction. The category works. It does not have a household name yet.

The Pattern

Categories that ended up reshaping investing always looked invisible first.

The pattern repeats. A structural truth exists. The math works. The capital is already moving in small ways. But the broader market has not named the category yet, so most allocators cannot see it. By the time they see it, the early movers have already compounded.

Index Funds
1976 to 1991 · 15 years to take hold

Jack Bogle launched the first retail index fund in 1976. Wall Street called it "Bogle's folly." The idea that you matched the market instead of beating it was treated as either obvious or insulting depending on who you asked.

Today, index funds hold trillions and are the default allocation for most retail capital.
Private Credit
2008 to 2024 · 16 years to scale

After the 2008 financial crisis, banks pulled back from middle-market lending. Apollo, Ares, and Blackstone Credit stepped in. For a decade, allocators called it niche.

By 2024, private credit had grown into a 1.7 trillion dollar asset class and was treated as a core institutional allocation.

Both stories share a structural shape. Someone built the category first. The market took years to recognize what was being built. The early participants compounded while the rest of the market was still asking what to call it.

There is a useful pattern at work in each of these stories. Investors who waited for the rest of the market to validate index funds missed the first 15 years of compounding. Investors who waited for institutions to embrace private credit missed the decade after 2008. One could even argue something as polarizing as Bitcoin from 2011 to 2025 fits the same pattern. Unfamiliar is not unsafe. Unfamiliar is unfamiliar. The work is to understand the structure and then decide.

That is the work this newsletter is built to help you do.

What Totem Is

A simple structure, in plain language.

Totem LC Group operates a commercial funding program. The mechanics are straightforward enough to explain in a paragraph.

A vetted vendor receives a purchase order from a Fortune 100 buyer. Amazon. Bank of America. UPS. CVS Health. Boeing. Toyota Motor North America. The vendor needs capital to fulfill that order. Banks are slow. Predatory private lenders are the alternative most of these vendors get pushed toward. Totem fills the gap. We finance the purchase order. The goods get produced and shipped. The buyer pays the vendor on standard 30 to 60 day terms. The funds release through a joint account. The capital partner who funded the transaction gets repaid principal plus a flat 5 percent origination fee. The cycle closes at 90 to 100 days.

The structure is what allows the protection layers to stack. Every transaction sits behind trade credit insurance from Allianz, with buyer-specific coverage endorsements that name the capital partner as the first-position beneficiary. Every transaction is also backed by a performance guarantee equal to 100 percent of principal plus the origination fee, in place before any capital moves. Joint account controls earmark capital to a specific purchase order. The goods financed are consumable, not for resale, which means the buyer needs them and pays for them.

The result is a capital deployment structure where both the principal and the return are contractually insured. As far as we have been able to find, no other product in the U.S. capital markets currently offers this combination.

Totem is the only place, that we know of, where capital can be deployed into productive U.S. commerce with 100% of principal AND 100% of returns insured by Allianz.

Not principal alone. Not returns alone. Both. Backed by the largest trade credit insurance company in the world. Tied to verified Fortune 100 purchase orders. Repaid on a 90 to 100 day cycle. Verifiable on the phone with our partners before any capital moves.

We make that claim publicly because if we are wrong, we want to know. If a similar structure exists somewhere, we want to study it. We have searched the public web, the institutional platforms, the private credit funds, and the major newsletters. We have not found one. That is what The Totem Challenge is built to test, week after week.

Where Totem Fits

Not your retirement. Not your home. Somewhere new.

Totem is not a replacement for any of the foundational pieces of a portfolio. Not a 401k. Not a primary residence. Not the equity index funds that anchor most long-term wealth-building plans. Those tools exist for reasons that still hold up.

What Totem fits next to is the part of capital most people do not think about much: the cash sitting in a savings account at 0.4 percent because nobody knows what else to do with it. The CD ladder paying 4 percent with three to twelve month terms. The brokerage cash earning a fraction. The home equity not generating productive yield. The money parked in a money market fund waiting for the next decision.

The audience for Totem is the investor or family who has done the foundational work, has capital that is currently idle or under-deployed, and is open to learning about a category they have not heard of. That is most of the people who will read this newsletter.

The Question Worth Asking
When was the last time your CDs, your savings, your bonds, or your idle cash insured 100% of your principal AND 100% of your returns?
For most readers, the honest answer is the same word. Never.

That question is the lens this newsletter applies to every category we cover going forward. Not as a gotcha. As a real test. Most products in the universe protect one side of the equation, or the other, or neither. The Totem structure is the only one we have found that protects both.
